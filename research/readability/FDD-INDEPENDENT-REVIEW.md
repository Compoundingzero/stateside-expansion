## Actionable qualifications

1. **Item 15 — distinguish unconditional manager-restriction disclosure from the conditional supervisor list.** Current §436.5(o)(2) begins “If personal ‘on-premises’ supervision is not required”; (o)(3) separately requires disclosure of restrictions the franchisee must place on its manager. The rewrite places the latter sentence inside the paragraph beginning with the (o)(2) condition. This can make the independent obligation look conditional. Suggested structure: keep the four conditional supervisor subjects together, including the individual-franchisee recommendation, then put manager restrictions in a separate sentence/paragraph: “Separately, disclose any restrictions the franchisee must place on its manager, such as the specified trade-secret or noncompetition restrictions.” Preserve enforceability limits; disclosure is not a ruling that a restriction is valid.

2. **Item 19 — make the document-location and no-claim choice explicit.** The draft correctly requires reasonable basis and written substantiation at the time a representation is made, but does not clearly state the additional requirement to state it in Item 19. The choice not to make a representation does not authorise representatives to provide ordinary oral/off-document earnings claims. The prescribed no-representation statement expressly says employees/representatives are not authorised to make them orally or in writing. Explain the limited existing-outlet actual-record route and compliant written supplemental representation separately; do not replace those exceptions with a blanket claim that no information outside the document is ever permitted. Current §436.5(s)(1)–(5).

3. **Item 6 — restore fee increases and define the calculation base.** The original specifically asked who can change fees and how a formula/cap is disclosed. Current §436.5(f), footnote 3, requires the increase formula **or** maximum increase amount where fees may increase; it also requires defining gross sales when used. The new general question “What number is the formula applied to?” is useful but loses that precise condition. Add a short explanation that the contractual gross-sales base may differ from cash collected or accounting revenue, and that disclosed increase powers/formulas/caps belong in the budget.

4. **Item 5 — restore the independent payment-timing question.** The original explains that calling an early payment a reservation fee does not settle its effect on disclosure timing. The rewrite thoroughly explains payees and refund terms but drops this caution. One sentence and the existing `fed-timing` citation can tell a beginner to check the actual payment against the independently applicable timing rule before collecting it. This is not an assertion that every payment to every third party has the same trigger.

## Useful precision in the priority articles

5. **Item 7 — prevent double counting and distinguish estimate mechanics.** The new $80,000/$100,000/$10,000 example is arithmetically correct and explicitly fictional. The original management instruction not to count an initial working-capital allowance twice has disappeared. Restore it: establish whether the startup total already includes the disclosed additional-funds allowance before adding a separate cash-gap amount. Current (g)(1)(iii) covers expenses before opening and during the stated initial period; the draft correctly preserves “at least three months or a reasonable period for the industry.” Avoid changing that wording into an unconditional three-month rule. A low/high range is based on current experience when the amount is unknown; existing original checklist range/basis detail could also be retained.

6. **Item 21 — explain conditional statement and guarantee branches, without implying universal eligibility.** There is no identified false statement in the current summary: its references to applicable audit/other requirements are deliberately broad. For the beginner’s first pass, however, the ordinary periods are worth stating: two previous year-end balance sheets and three previous years of operations, stockholders’ equity and cash flows. The start-up-system phase-in has specific conditions; a new selling subsidiary is not automatically a qualifying start-up system. If discussing use of affiliate statements instead, specify the absolute/unconditional guarantee covering all franchisor obligations to the franchisee and attachment requirement; an ordinary support letter does not establish that alternative. Parents committing post-sale performance or guaranteeing obligations and subfranchisors have their separate statement branch. The current U.S. GAAP/SEC-permitted formulation matches the XML; do not substitute a blanket foreign-audit acceptance claim. Current (u)(1)–(2).

7. **Item 23 — add a few concrete receipt facts.** The draft accurately distinguishes delivery acknowledgment from investment approval or a general waiver. It safely says to read the complete prescribed item, but beginners would benefit from the direct instruction that two prescribed detachable receipts go at the end of the document. Required information includes each franchise seller’s name, principal address and telephone number, issuance date, the registered-agent information if not already disclosed as specified, exhibit titles and signature/date space. Current (w). No new universal retention period or receipt-as-waiver claim should be invented from this item alone.

8. **Item 22 — retain the changed-document timing handoff.** The original says late changes to fees, guarantees and related terms need the separate material-change/timing review. The rewrite keeps version control but could restore this explicit handoff so the novice does not think a complete index alone makes the latest signing pack ready. The original already cites `fed-timing`; keep the exact trigger analysis separate rather than inventing a new universal waiting period.

## Readability and sample clearances

- Items 1/5 now define affiliate through all three control relationships; “franchisor” is a business, not only an incorporated company.
- Item 18’s relevant-geographic-area public-figure definition and the distinction between compensation/benefits for franchise-name/symbol or franchise-offer endorsement and ordinary consumer promotion match current (r)(1)–(4). The example does not invent operational responsibility from celebrity visibility.
- Item 19’s fictional calculation is correct: $1,000,000 × 6% = $60,000; $180,000 − $120,000 − $60,000 = $0 before further omitted costs. The existing warning not to subtract already-included costs is helpful.
- Item 20’s 20 + 8 − 5 = 23/net growth 3 is correct and expressly simplified. “Leave the counted population” avoids asserting that every transfer removes an operating outlet or that every departure is failure.
- Items 3/4 preserve allegation-versus-finding and historical-versus-present-condition distinctions. Nothing makes an old proceeding a guaranteed prediction of failure or a blank search proof of no dispute.
- Items 8/13/14/16 preserve separation of private brand rights, ownership/permission and independently applicable product, use or operating rules. Items 9/17 preserve the difference between cross-reference summaries and actual enforceable obligations.
- The identical approximately 35-word checklist introduction appears in all 23 articles. It is harmless legally, but can be deleted or customised to follow the instruction against generic wrappers and leave more room for the article-specific decision. Headings may be consistently structured without repeating this paragraph.
- Some articles use **affiliate**, **agent**, **covenant**, or **cure** without an in-article explanation. A short first-use definition is more useful than expecting the reader to have read another item. Avoid introducing additional legal jargon while applying the precise repairs above.
