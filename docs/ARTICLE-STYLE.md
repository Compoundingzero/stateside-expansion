# Explain the rules before asking the reader to use them

ELN serves owners and managers of established European and Southeast Asian
businesses considering U.S. expansion. Assume the reader is intelligent and can
follow a first-year university explanation, but has no prior U.S. legal training.
Do not write the main article as a research note or adviser handoff.

Start with the business situation: who is doing what, where and why the question
arises. Define the people, agencies and unfamiliar words before relying on them.
Explain both what a rule says and what it means for the proposed business. Keep
material conditions and exceptions; simplification must not become a new rule.

Use a developed worked example. Walk through the mistaken assumption, the actual
facts to collect, how the requirement applies to those facts and the practical
consequence. Label invented businesses and figures as fictional. For documented
cases, distinguish allegations, admissions, judgments and proposed settlements.
An ordered payment is not evidence that money was recovered.

Each article has a short `learningSummary` and two to six `keyTerms` entries, in
addition to its actual teaching sections. Define terms inline where needed too.
Do not attach a generic preface to an otherwise unexplained technical article.
Use enough detail to teach; a word target is not a reason to pad or drop facts.

Replace research shorthand with its meaning. For example, explain a 'release
gate' as the particular action that must be completed before signing or opening,
and an 'evidence chain' as the actual records showing who applied and what was
issued. Explain 'cohort' as the group of businesses included in a comparison.
Do not alter identifiers, legal references or URLs through prose-cleanup scripts.

Separate the rule from optional management preparation. A checklist can help a
team collect facts without becoming a legal filing or an automatic approval.
Keep federal, state, territorial, home-country and local responsibilities distinct.
Show important limits in ordinary words without repeating a warning after every
paragraph. Detailed rule records and source-audit notes remain available below
an accessible explanation; they should not dominate the first reading.

Use official sources for legal claims. The FTC's May 2008 Compliance Guide is a
useful explanatory reference with worked examples, but current Part 436 and later
official updates govern current requirements and amounts. Do not import an old
threshold or accounting discussion as current law. Relevant links:

- https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/franchise-rule-compliance-guide
- https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/franchise-rule
- https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/bus70-franchise-rule-compliance-guide.pdf
- https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-436

Editorial changes update `contentModifiedAt`; they do not automatically refresh
source-review dates, legal statuses or publication decisions. Preserve stable
IDs, routes and source relationships. The readability revision's input snapshots
and patches are under `research/readability/`; `scripts/apply-readability.mjs`
checks allowed fields and citation scope before applying them.
