## 1. Define the audience precisely

The primary customer is an **owner of an established business whose managers already run its day-to-day operations**. The owner is considering taking the existing business model into the United States. Secondary users include the CEO, COO, CFO, export manager, and expansion director acting for that owner.

Do not confuse this customer with someone looking to buy an American franchise, purchase a job, obtain passive income, or relocate through an investment visa. Immigration becomes a relevant workstream when someone actually intends to work in the United States.

Build for businesses with an existing offer, customers, delivery capability, management responsibilities, and financial records. Do not invent a statutory revenue threshold for “SMB.” Use both “SME” and “SMB” naturally where appropriate to the audience.

Treat management independence as something to investigate. A company may have branch managers but still depend on its owner for sales, technical decisions, key accounts, supplier negotiations, recruitment, or quality control. Help users document these dependencies without pretending that a questionnaire proves transferability.

Give substantial attention to **B2B services**: commercial cleaning, facilities services, pest control, industrial maintenance, technical services, training, IT services, and other repeatable business services. Support relevant consumer-facing sectors through separate branches. Do not let restaurant examples define the whole product.

For B2B companies, address procurement cycles, account concentration, recurring contracts, receivables, bid requirements, route density, insurance, service-level obligations, and national-account allocation.

Use straightforward English. Define American terms at first use. Avoid generic growth claims, inflated promises, startup jargon, invented testimonials, and claims that expansion can be effortless or passive.

## 2. Establish the research boundaries before writing

Treat any supplied outline as a set of research leads. Verify its claims rather than copying them into the site.

Apply these corrections and safeguards:

- **Federal definition and exemptions:** The federal franchise definition, federal exemptions, and state definitions are separate analyses. The current minimum-payment exemption applies where total required payments or commitments to make required payments to the franchisor or an affiliate, made any time before through six months after commencing operations, are less than $735. The older $500 figure is not the current exemption amount. Recheck the current rule when building; do not hard-code this as a universal franchise-definition threshold. Source: [16 CFR §436.8](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-436/subpart-E/section-436.8).
- **Significant control or assistance:** This depends on the actual relationship and overall method of operation. A manual, approved supplier, or software requirement is a fact to examine, not an automatic answer. Separate trademark quality protection from broader operating controls. Source: [FTC Franchise Rule Compliance Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/franchise-rule-compliance-guide).
- **Single-license treatment:** The single-trademark-license exclusion is narrow. Never describe it as one free franchise, one exempt outlet, or one exempt license per state. Check current federal guidance and independent state treatment. Historical staff opinions must be identified as such. Starting source: [FTC staff opinion 05-1](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/advisory-opinions/informal-staff-advisory-opinion-05-1).
- **State obligations:** Separate registration, notice filing, exemptions, disclosure, advertising, and relationship laws. Do not build a simple “14 registration states versus everywhere else” answer or a universal ban on discovery calls. Analyse the relevant offer, sale, parties, locations, and communications. For example, Texas describes a pre-offer exemption notice for qualifying FTC-compliant franchisors: [Texas business-opportunity FAQs](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/statdoc/faqs2700.shtml).
- **Disclosure timing:** Explain federal disclosure timing, including the usual 14-calendar-day period before a binding agreement or payment, and separately research agreement changes and state overlays. Source: [16 CFR §436.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-436/subpart-B/section-436.2).
- **Financial assurance:** Requirements are not all secret. Distinguish published requirements from documented case-specific examiner requests and practitioner observations. California publishes examples including guarantees, escrow, bonds, fee deferral, and capital infusion: [California application instructions](https://dfpi.ca.gov/regulated-industries/franchises/instructions-on-how-to-file-a-complete-franchise-application/).
- **Unsupported benchmarks:** Do not repeat unsupported percentages for omitted earnings information, occupancy-cost uplifts, or permit delays. Item 19 financial performance representations are optional; that is different from omitting the disclosure item. Explain the limitations of revenue figures and benchmark cohorts. Source: [FTC guide to buying a franchise](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumers-guide-buying-franchise).
- **Leases:** Model actual lease terms. Do not assume every American commercial lease is NNN or that additional charges equal a fixed percentage. Separate base rent, taxes, insurance, maintenance, utilities, deposits, improvements, and reconciliation exposure.
- **Court records:** PACER provides a nationwide federal court index, including bankruptcy; state and local searches remain separate. Never equate “no result found” with “no disputes.” Source: [PACER national index](https://pacer.uscourts.gov/find-case/search-national-index).
- **Entity and immigration structure:** Do not recommend an LLC or corporation as a universal immigration solution. Keep entity form, tax treatment, ownership/control, qualifying corporate relationships, and individual eligibility separate. A licensing or franchise agreement should not be assumed to establish an L-1 qualifying relationship. Research [8 CFR §214.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/subpart-A/section-214.2) and [USCIS ownership and control guidance](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-l-chapter-5).
- **E-2 eligibility:** Distinguish incorporation, ultimate ownership nationality, control, the person’s role, and treaty conditions. Check country-specific footnotes and qualifying investment or escrow requirements. Do not suggest that incorporating in a treaty country creates eligibility. Sources: [Treaty countries](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/treaty.html) and [State Department E-visa guidance](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09fam/09fam040209.html).
- **Consequences:** Explain possible remedies and penalties with jurisdiction and procedural context. Do not promise that every violation freezes a business or requires repayment of every dollar invested.

The website provides education, issue identification, and preparation. It must not issue legal clearance, approve an exemption, determine visa eligibility, certify a partner, or imply that completing a checklist authorizes a sale.

Identify specific unresolved questions that require professional judgment; do not replace useful explanations with repeated “consult a lawyer” paragraphs.

Record a status for **each jurisdiction and relevant topic family**; a complete franchise-law matrix does not establish complete tax, employment, sector, or local coverage.

### U.S. coverage

Include:

- Federal requirements and **all 50 states plus Washington, D.C.**
- A separately identified territorial workstream for Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, and American Samoa. Do not silently treat territories as states or apply mainland assumptions.
- State, county, city, and relevant special-jurisdiction overlays for each researched operating location.
- Commercial regions that help users compare markets, clearly distinguished from lawmaking jurisdictions.

For local operating requirements, start with **30–50 researched markets**, then expand using a documented queue. Select markets for customer fit and source availability; do not label them “best” without a stated method.

Within a metro, identify the actual municipality, county, and address-dependent issues. A Los Angeles metro page cannot establish the rules for every city in that metro.

### Origin coverage

- All Southeast Asian countries: Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Timor-Leste, and Vietnam.

- Detailed guides prioritized by distinctive cross-border issues, supported demand, and adequate evidence. Every registered origin needs a coverage status, even if a standalone page is not yet justified.

Keep these facts separate:

**Parent incorporation; headquarters; actual operations; owners’ nationality; tax residence; U.S. selling entity; buyer residence; proposed outlet location; where offers are made or received; imported product; employee work location; and commercial model.**

## 6. Build one guided journey that produces useful work

The output is a private **U.S. Expansion Brief** that the owner can give to their management team and advisers. It should contain actual decisions, assumptions, responsibilities, and unresolved questions.

Use the following default stages, but make dependencies adaptive. Immigration, imports, ownership, or professional licensing can move earlier when they affect feasibility.

| Stage | Main question | Output | Example of the single forward CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Management | Can the business deliver without the owner’s daily involvement? | Responsibility map and owner dependencies | Map our management team |
| 2. U.S. model | What will the partner receive, pay, and be required to do? | Relationship factsheet and responsibility diagram | Describe our U.S. model |
| 3. First market | Where should the first operation be investigated? | Shortlist, demand assumptions, and jurisdiction map | Compare our first markets |
| 4. Economics | Can the operating unit and support system both work? | Unit, brand-support, and cash-flow models | Test our first unit |
| 5. Partner | What capability, funding, and commitments are required? | Partner evidence checklist and territory principles | Build our partner brief |
| 6. Launch requirements | What must happen before each commitment? | Tasks, dependencies, responsible people, and evidence | Build our launch plan |
| 7. Decision | What is supported, and what remains unresolved? | Exportable Expansion Brief | Create our expansion brief |

Ask one question or one closely related input group at a time.

Begin with low-friction context: home market, business activity, management setup, and expansion intent. Ask for more only when it changes the next task. Support “not decided” and “not known.”

Distinguish unit operation, area development through the developer’s own units, master arrangements with subfranchising, area representation, distribution, and IP licensing.

Draw who contracts with whom, who pays whom, and who trains, recruits, supports, and monitors whom. Labels do not settle legal classification.

Make pre-offer, pre-signature/payment, pre-employment/import, pre-opening, and ongoing requirements visible at the relevant point. Do not hide early sales-law issues until an end-of-journey checklist.

Let users explore later stages and public guides while identifying unresolved dependencies. A permit tied to an unselected address should be recorded as pending location selection, not treated as already satisfied.

Before broad territory commitments, distinguish demonstrated U.S. customer demand and delivery capability from assumptions transferred from the home market. Identify the smallest practical validation activity and keep untested assumptions visible.

The final brief should include:

- Proposed model and management dependencies.
- Chosen markets and demand evidence.
- Three financial scenarios and funding needs.
- Partner requirements and territory principles.
- Regulatory tasks and responsible people.
- Ownership and mobility questions.
- Source dates, unresolved issues, and named next actions.

Valid outcomes include further investigation, a U.S. pilot, a revised model, a different entry route, or a pause. An incomplete brief can still be exported with its gaps clearly shown.

### Original scenarios to author in full

**A. The Singapore cleaning company’s U.S. unit whose apparent profit disappears**

Use an explicitly fictional annual model:

- $1,000,000 revenue.
- $180,000 operating surplus before a dedicated general manager and royalty.
- $120,000 fully loaded manager.
- A 6% revenue royalty.

The manager and royalty consume that $180,000. There is then no surplus at that stage for other omitted obligations.

Show the arithmetic and identify what was already included in the original costs. Do not present these inputs as market averages or earnings representations for a real franchise.

The reader identifies missing management costs, then creates a properly labelled U.S. unit model.

**B. The German technical-services company with a “license”**

The partner receives a brand, operating procedures, software requirements, training, and continuing support, and pays fees.

Show why the title of the agreement cannot answer the classification question. Have the reader build a relationship factsheet rather than receive an automated legal verdict.

**C. The British facilities company and the wealthy partner**

Use fictional figures:

- $4 million net worth.
- $200,000 accessible unencumbered funding.
- $250,000 launch costs.
- A further $150,000 reserve.

The demonstrated funding gap is $200,000 unless other committed capital is established.

Teach the difference between net worth, liquidity, source of funds, competing commitments, and capital actually available to this project.

**D. The Dutch industrial-services brand granting a large territory**

An operator requests several states before demonstrating one viable operation.

Teach staged rights, realistic development obligations, support commitments, national accounts, online leads, overlapping sales, cure provisions, and regulatory delays.

Protect the partner’s investment as well as the brand’s growth options. Do not promise protection from independent competitors or enforceability of unreviewed terms.

**E. The Malaysian-owned group with a Singapore subsidiary**

The team assumes the subsidiary’s address decides E-2 eligibility.

Reveal the missing ownership, nationality, control, personnel, and investment facts. Let the user produce an ownership diagram and a people-mobility plan. Do not require passport uploads for this learning task.

**F. The French operator who can run branches but cannot support other owners**

The candidate is excellent at delivery but lacks subfranchise recruitment, disclosure processes, training capacity, and field support.

Compare the responsibilities of an area developer and a master operator. Produce a capability-and-cost matrix.

**G. The restaurant whose lease clock starts before permissions are settled**

Use a fictional location and clearly labelled delay assumptions.

Demonstrate rent commencement, fit-out, use approval, inspection dependencies, and cash burn. Link actual local-law explanations only to a named, researched jurisdiction.

**H. The successful home-market product that cannot be supplied identically**

Use an industry-specific import or sourcing problem to teach specifications, regulatory responsibility, local substitutes, minimum orders, landed costs, and brand consistency.

Do not invent an FDA or USDA approval requirement; research the exact product category.

Every case must finish with a reusable decision or evidence checklist. Keep the interactive version optional and provide a readable text version.

## 10. Implement practical tools

Build tools that work and explain their assumptions:

1. **Management-dependence mapper:** Responsibilities, evidence, owner-only tasks, and coverage gaps.
2. **Relationship factsheet:** IP, payments, controls, assistance, counterparties, and jurisdictions; issue flags without a binary legal classification.
3. **Market comparison:** Customer fit, operating requirements, sourced cost inputs, and unresolved facts. Use transparent user-selected weights if scoring is offered; unknown data must not score as favourable.
4. **U.S. unit-economics calculator:** Management pay, payroll burden, labour, occupancy, insurance, materials, royalties, marketing, local sales, and operating overhead. Distinguish operating profit from cash flow, debt service, taxes, and reinvestment.
5. **Brand and master-support economics:** Royalty allocation, withholding assumptions, training, field support, travel, recruitment, localisation, compliance, and overhead. Show whether continuing support is funded without assuming endless upfront territory sales.
6. **Cash-runway calculator:** Opening delays, deposits, pre-opening expenditure, working capital, receivables, inventory, debt, and reserve assumptions. Derive funding needs from dated cash flows; avoid double counting expenses and reserves.
7. **FDD comparison workspace:** Source year, entity, Items 5–8, 11–12, 15, 17, 19–21, cohort definitions, exclusions, and missing information. 8. **Partner evidence checklist:** Accessible capital, allocation, operating experience, management capacity, references, entity records, federal/state court coverage, and subfranchise-support ability. Record evidence dates and limitations; do not certify character or funding from self-reported answers.
9. **Launch dependency planner and Expansion Brief export:** Responsible person, prerequisite, source, status, date, and next action; usable printable/PDF output and data export.

Use actual user inputs or visibly labelled fictional defaults. Record currency, period, location, date, and whether each input is a quote, historical figure, official statistic, or assumption.

Include downside, base, and upside scenarios without pretending they are forecasts.

Keep a private planning calculator separate from marketing claims to prospective franchisees. Do not automatically publish a user’s projections or turn them into sales promises.

## 14. Engineering, privacy, and maintainability

Inspect the existing project before choosing a stack. Use a maintainable framework with prerendering/server rendering for the public reference site and lightweight interactive tools.

Keep research records, content rendering, calculation logic, and private plans separate.

The initial tools should use transparent deterministic logic. Do not require a general-purpose AI chat interface or live model call to read a rule, calculate a cost, or continue the journey.

Build a content administration workflow with source records, draft/review/published states, conflicts, near-duplicate review, stale-content alerts, revisions, and a coverage dashboard.

Claims and sources should be traceable both ways.

Keep private plans out of public URLs, search indexes, analytics payloads, logs, and shared caches. Use authentication and access controls for server-stored plans; `noindex` alone is not privacy protection.

Permit anonymous exploration with minimal non-sensitive local progress, and provide a clear reset/export path.

Avoid collecting passports, bank statements, personal court records, or confidential contracts for the educational experience.

If later document storage is needed, implement the security, permissions, retention, deletion, and consent controls before enabling uploads.

Research the site’s own applicable privacy and cookie obligations for its actual operation and target markets. Minimize tracking. Do not weaken necessary consent or accessibility controls to satisfy the single-CTA design.

Respect source access conditions and document rights. Link public FDDs and verify extracts rather than republishing entire third-party documents by default.

Do not bypass access controls or fabricate data where a source is unavailable.
